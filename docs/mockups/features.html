Pick your features

You choose what Islet does, and it only asks for what those features need. Search gets a clear choice between taking over ⌘Space or leaving Spotlight and Siri where they are. Everything here is clickable, and the switches change what this Mac has allowed.

This Mac:

Features first, then permissions

  • Setup starts with a checklist of features. Each one says what it needs. Untick Notifications and Messages, and Full Disk Access, Contacts and Control Messages are never asked for.
  • The next step only lists what you picked, grouped by feature, with each line ticking itself off as you allow it.
  • In Settings, the Islet page lists each feature with a switch and one state: Ready, Off, or what it still needs. A feature that's off is never an issue and never gets a badge.
  • “Can use” permissions never nag. Contacts for people in search, or Location for Wi-Fi names, show as a quiet “Allow to also…” line, not a warning.

Search and Siri

  • Two plain choices: “Use ⌘Space for Islet” (Spotlight moves to ⌥Space) or “Keep ⌘Space for Spotlight” (Islet uses another shortcut). The first walks you through the System Settings change and ticks it off when it sees it.
  • Siri: apps can’t ask Siri directly, but Siri lives in Spotlight, and on your Mac Spotlight sends a typed question to Siri on Return. Islet does both: it opens Spotlight, types your question and presses Return, so Siri is already answering.
  • When Siri comes first: text starting with “siri” (any case; the word itself is dropped), a question (what, how, when, why, who, can, should, is…, or ending in “?”), or nothing Islet can find. Otherwise “Ask Siri” waits in More, next to Spotlight and the web.
  • Needs Accessibility, to type into Spotlight. Without it, Ask Siri isn’t offered.

To decide

  1. Should the setup checklist start with everything ticked, or with only the island ticked?
  2. Should Settings hide the pages of features that are off, or show them dimmed with a switch at the top, as here?
  3. Should a question Islet can’t answer go to Siri on Return, or should Return still open the top app or file when there is one? Here, a question always goes to Siri first.
