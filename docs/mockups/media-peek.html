Peek at hidden media

Media swiped away, pointer over the notch: the island picks up the colours of the album art. With the glass look the glass strip is tinted; without it a thin line glows under the camera. Swipe down and that colour becomes the artwork. Swipe left or right to skip: the indicator follows your fingers, and letting go finishes at the speed you were going.

How the skip gesture feels

  • Rolling the name: the song's name sits in the bar under the notch, the next song just off to the right and the previous just off to the left. Your fingers roll it: 80 pt of travel brings the neighbour all the way in, and past that it rubber-bands. The new name shows as "Next song" or "Previous song" until it lands, since the player only says it then.
  • The wings: skip plays in the right wing, where the app icon shrinks away; rewind in the left, where the artwork does. The icon grows with your fingers, and when the song changes its arrows push one step forward.
  • Half way: once the neighbour is half in, its icon lights up and the trackpad taps. Pull back past half and it un-lights.
  • Letting go: on the frame your fingers lift, a spring takes the strip from exactly where it is at exactly the speed it was moving. Past half way, or flung in, the neighbour lands in the slot with a slight settle; short of it, or flung back, the current song slides home. A flick lands in about 0.2 s; a slow release eases in over about 0.35 s. The song changes the moment it lands.
  • New colours: the next song's artwork isn't known until the player switches. On the peek the colour never moves; it fades to the new song's colours in place when the new artwork arrives.
  • One song per swipe: the trackpad's coasting after you lift doesn't count, so a long flick can't skip twice. In the app, the scroll event's phase says exactly when the fingers lift, so there's no waiting for the coast to end.
  • Direction: fingers left is next, fingers right is previous, following natural scrolling. It flips with the system setting.
  • Colours: two or three main colours from the artwork, saturated a touch so dull covers still read on black.
